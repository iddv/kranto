# Plain words

From Orwell's six rules (*Politics and the English Language*, 1946), Zinsser's *On Writing
Well*, the GOV.UK style guide and plainlanguage.gov.

1. **Write the short word a friend would use.** "Use", not "utilise"; "help", not
   "facilitate"; "work with", not "collaborate"; "start", not "initiate".
2. **Cut every word that does no work.** Not: "in order to", "it is important to note that",
   "the fact that", "at this point in time".
3. **Name who does what.** Active voice by default. Not: "mistakes were made"; write "the team
   shipped the bug".
4. **One idea a sentence; about 15 to 20 words on average,** some much shorter.
5. **Write for a reader at about grade 8** unless the person's voice card asks for more. Your
   reader follows the topic: use the simpler word rather than stopping to define a term.
6. **Prefer a concrete example to an abstraction.** Not: "significant performance gains";
   write "pages load in 0.4 s instead of 2 s".
7. **Write numbers as digits** (7, 40%, $3) and dates in full the first time.
8. **Use sentence case for headings,** without emoji or bold labels.
9. **Never use a metaphor you are used to seeing in print.** Not: "a perfect storm", "move the
   needle", "low-hanging fruit".
